A SMART Recommendation is an OIML Recommendation rendered as a structured,
executable model: the same source drives the page you read, the forms the
laboratory fills, and the verdicts the platform computes. The published
text stays authoritative; the model is its machine-actionable companion,
with every element traceable to the clause it came from.

## What you can do today

- **Read a Recommendation as a structured document.** [OIML R 60](/recommendations/r60), [R 129](/recommendations/r129), and [R 144](/recommendations/r144) render on this site with their modelled requirement and test structure, clause-referenced throughout.
- **Walk any statement back to the adopted text.** Every content element carries its source clause (for example `urn:oiml:pub:r:60-1:2021`, clause 5.2); the R 60 page shows the references inline.
- **Browse the modelled set.** The [SMART Recommendations site](https://www.oimlsmart.org/recs/) lists the pilot Recommendation (R 60, load cells) and the demo models (R 91, R 129, R 144), with the authoring documentation for new ones.
- **Open the package as data.** The [studio viewer](https://www.oimlsmart.org/studio/view/) renders the published models as a navigable tree: the roles, the processes, the requirements; click any element to inspect its properties (the capture above).
- **Watch the model run a certification.** The [live demo](https://demo.oimlsmart.org/) certifies a simulated load cell against the modelled R 60, end to end; sign in with a one-click demo account, no password.
- **Consume the model in your own tooling.** Every surface exports as generated projections: ReqIF for requirements-management tools, RDF/OWL with SHACL shapes for linked-data consumers, OpenCDD back-references for attribute definitions.

## How it works

Each Recommendation is authored as a Primmel package: the instrument as a
subject with its anatomy, the requirements as constraints bound to the
subject's aspects, the conformance tests that verify each requirement, and
the report forms whose fields bind into the same graph. The packages are
the single source of truth; the data trees the platform serves are
generated artifacts, and a drift guard proves both directions byte-clean,
so what the platform runs is what was authored.

The authority stays where the OIML process puts it. The committee's
decision is the text; the model is derived from it and checked against it,
never the reverse.

## Why it exists

A Recommendation as published is prose. Every tool that touches it, the
editor, the test-bench software, the certificate template, re-encodes the
text by hand, and every re-encoding drifts a little from the document the
committee adopted. Two laboratories reading the same clause can derive two
different test procedures, and the divergence surfaces only when the
results are compared. The model removes the re-encoding: the reading, the
testing, and the verdict cannot drift apart.

## The honest questions

**Does the model replace the published Recommendation?**
No. The published text stays authoritative; the model is its
machine-actionable companion. Every modelled element carries its clause
reference precisely so the derivation can be audited against the adopted
text, and the platform's gates fail when the two drift apart.

**Which Recommendations are covered?**
R 60 (the pilot, load cells), with R 91, R 129, and R 144 as demo models.
New Recommendations follow the authoring documentation on the
[SMART Recommendations site](https://www.oimlsmart.org/recs/); the
committee's adoption process does not change.

**What happens when a Recommendation is revised?**
Editions are first-class in the underlying language: the package pins its
source edition, and the reference toolchain's model diff shows exactly
what a revision changes, so downstream deployments regenerate rather than
patch by hand.

**Do I need an account, or software, to read one?**
Neither. The pages render on the open web, and the studio viewer runs in
the browser with no sign-in.
