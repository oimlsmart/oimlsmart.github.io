The SST (Simulated SMART Twin) is a family of simulated measuring
instruments with realistic physics, built for training and rehearsal: the
complete certification workflow runs against them with no hardware
involved. Each simulated instrument serves the same governed twin
interface that a physical SMART instrument serves, so software, and
people, cannot tell the difference from the served side.

## Why it exists

Certification skills are learned on instruments that are expensive,
scarce, and slow to fail in instructive ways. Platform changes need a
rehearsal space where a verdict chain can be exercised end to end before
it touches a real evaluation. And a certification system that is never
shown a failing instrument cannot demonstrate what failure looks like.
The simulation supplies instruments that behave honestly, fail on
purpose, and reset in seconds.

## How it works

Each simulated instrument presents two channels. The `/twin` channel is
the governed projection, and its schema is generated from the
manufacturer's product reference package; a startup conformance check
stops the instrument if the served interface drifts from the package. The
`/world` channel is the physical world: the operator places loads, sweeps
the environmental conditions, injects faults, and advances the virtual
clock. An epistemic wall separates the two: certification logic reads
only the served channel, and the world channel answers to the operator.
The bench renders the physical scene beside an analogue dial, a
ground-truth display a human reads with their eyes, which is how the
classroom teaches the lying-twin lesson: the API reports what it reports,
and the needle shows what is true.

Four instruments cover the four modelled Recommendations, each with its
own physics: a strain-gauge load cell (OIML R 60; creep and thermal
hysteresis), a continuous gas monitor (OIML R 144; drift classes and
cross-sensitivity), a Doppler speed meter (OIML R 91; cosine error and
rain fade, which lose readings rather than inventing them), and an
optical conveyor dimensioner (OIML R 129; sampling, reflectance, and
ambient-light noise). Named scenarios such as the creep-honest cell and
the deliberately drifting cell make the teaching cases repeatable.

## Where it runs live

The estate's reference rehearsal is the pilot arc: the ACME LC-500
product reference is type-evaluated against the modelled OIML R 60, the
certificate issues through the signing gate, a quarry deployment binds
one simulated twin, and the monitor runs a simulated quarter in which
drift flags the certificate, an outage degrades verdicts to
indeterminate, and a served fault opens a service case. The whole arc is
an executable proof in the platform's own gate set, and the same arc is
what the live demo runs.

## How it interops

The simulation is driven from the platform, not beside it: the platform's
gateway consumes the `/twin` channel through its standard connector
registry, and the interaction contract is two endpoints and four verbs.
Because the served schema is generated from the same product reference
packages the platform reads, the simulation proves the supply chain it
simulates: a package edit that changes the served interface fails the
instrument's own startup check.
