Primmel is the modelling language underneath the SMART Recommendations:
the language the models are authored in, checked in, and executed from.
It exists because no established standards format carries machine-executable
semantics, and the estate needed a standard that software can run, not
only display.

## Why it exists

A conformance clause carries several machine-relevant facts fused into
one sentence of prose. OIML R 60-1:2021, clause 5.2 names a subject (a
load cell), an aspect under test (the largest applied load), a reference
aspect (the maximum capacity), a constraint between them, and its own
provenance. Document-centric tooling can tag the paragraph as a
requirement, which makes the text findable. It does not make the
semantics executable: no tagged database answers "does this instrument
pass?" with a computed verdict. Primmel's claim is that a standard can
be modelled so that all of these facts are first-class, typed, and
related, and then the standard runs.

## How it works

Every statement in a Primmel model is one of three relations over eight
terms: a thing's identity (what it is), its attribution (what it has),
and its behavior (what it does), over objects, properties, values,
transitions, and processes. Requirements bind constraints to subject
aspects; conformance tests carry acceptance criteria that derive from the
requirements they verify; report forms bind their fields into the same
subject graph. Packages compose with explicit imports, editions, and
version pins, and a rule catalog (the linter's C-rules) checks every
package mechanically.

A model that cannot be validated, queried, executed, and reasoned over
is, in the language's own terms, a document in a costume. The claim is
falsifiable in public: the conformance suite pairs a versioned clause map
with a corpus of valid and invalid documents, and any implementation,
not only the reference toolchain, can run it through a documented
adapter contract.

## How it interops

Primmel is the kernel, not a wire format. The models export to ReqIF,
RDF/OWL with SHACL shapes, and OpenCDD back-references as generated
projections for ecosystems that do not run a Primmel engine; the
[interoperability page](/technologies/dataspace-interop) carries the map
and the doctrine. The language's own specification is published as a
draft for comment, and the conformance suite's clause identifiers re-key
to it as it publishes.

## Who may use it, and who may run it

The language is a public artifact, not a hosted service: the
specification and the conformance suite are published, the reference
kernel is MIT-licensed, and reading, implementing, or running any of it
attaches to no member category. Where members meet Primmel models on the
estate, the surface is the Studio viewer, whose determination is quoted
