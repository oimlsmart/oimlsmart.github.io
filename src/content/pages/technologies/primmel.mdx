Primmel is the open modelling language underneath the SMART
Recommendations: the language the models are authored in, checked in, and
executed from. It is developed by [Ribose](https://www.ribose.com) and its
partners as open work: the specification is published for comment, the
conformance suite is public and versioned, and the reference tooling is
MIT-licensed. OIML SMART is its reference adopter, the programme that
proves the language on real standards; the language itself is not an OIML
artifact and is locked to no organization.

## What you can do today

- **Read the language.** The [kernel volume](https://www.primmel.org/primmel-smart-docs/primmel/) covers subjects, processes, mappings, data and values, packages, provenance, and the interop projections; the [foundation volume](https://www.primmel.org/primmel-smart-docs/foundation/) carries the IS–HAS–DOES system with its proofs.
- **Inspect the reference kernel.** [primmel-ts](https://github.com/primmel/primmel-ts) is MIT-licensed: the parser, the linter's rule catalog, the coverage calculus, and the model diff.
- **Run the conformance suite against any implementation.** The [public suite](https://github.com/primmel/primmel-ts/tree/v1/conformance) pairs a versioned clause map with a corpus of valid and invalid documents; a documented adapter contract lets third-party implementations plug in.
- **Edit a package in the open tooling.** The [Primmel editor](https://github.com/primmel/editor) is MIT-licensed: packages as the unit of work, with provenance on every save.
- **See the language at work on a real standard.** The [studio viewer](https://www.oimlsmart.org/studio/view/) opens the modelled OIML Recommendations (the capture above); the models' content is Primmel packages all the way down.
- **Audit the design against the alternatives.** The [alternatives audit](https://www.primmel.org/primmel-smart-docs/shared/alternatives-audit/) records what the language adopted from the standards-body answers and what it deliberately rejected.
- **Export, never convert.** Models project to ReqIF, RDF/OWL with SHACL shapes, and OpenCDD back-references as generated artifacts for ecosystems that do not run a Primmel engine.

## How it works

Every statement in a Primmel model is one of three relations over eight
terms: a thing's identity (what it is), its attribution (what it has), and
its behavior (what it does), over objects, properties, values, transitions,
and processes. Requirements bind constraints to subject aspects;
conformance tests carry acceptance criteria that derive from the
requirements they verify; report forms bind their fields into the same
subject graph. Packages compose with explicit imports, editions, and
version pins, and a rule catalog (the linter's C-rules) checks every
package mechanically.

<PrimmelKernel />

A model that cannot be validated, queried, executed, and reasoned over is,
in the language's own terms, a document in a costume. The claim is
falsifiable in public: the conformance suite pairs a versioned clause map
with a corpus of valid and invalid documents, and any implementation, not
only the reference toolchain, can run it through a documented adapter
contract.

## Why it exists

A conformance clause carries several machine-relevant facts fused into one
sentence of prose. OIML R 60-1:2021, clause 5.2 names a subject (a load
cell), an aspect under test (the largest applied load), a reference aspect
(the maximum capacity), a constraint between them, and its own provenance.
Document-centric tooling can tag the paragraph as a requirement, which
makes the text findable. It does not make the semantics executable: no
tagged database answers "does this instrument pass?" with a computed
verdict. Primmel's claim is that a standard can be modelled so that all of
these facts are first-class, typed, and related, and then the standard
runs.

## Who may use it, and who may run it

The language is a public artifact, not a hosted service, and not an
OIML-locked one: the specification and the conformance suite are
published, the reference kernel and the editor are MIT-licensed, and
reading, implementing, or running any of it attaches to no member
category. Where members meet Primmel models on the estate, the surface is

## The honest questions

**Why not an existing language?**
The two serious standards-body answers were audited on their corpora, not
their brochures: the DIN DKE SPEC 99200 ReqIF profile and the IEC-ISO Core
Ontology. Both stop at the document's text; neither can say what a
requirement constrains, whether something conforms, or how conformity is
established. Primmel adopted what each does better and deliberately
rejected the rest; the [audit record](https://www.primmel.org/primmel-smart-docs/shared/alternatives-audit/)
is public, section by section.

**Is Primmel an OIML invention?**
No. Primmel is developed by Ribose and its partners as open work, with the
specification, the conformance suite, and the MIT-licensed tooling public.
OIML SMART is the reference adopter: the programme builds on open work
rather than inventing a private one, which is the answer to the
not-invented-here concern in both directions.

**Does adopting it lock the programme to one vendor?**
No. The specification is public, the conformance suite is
implementation-neutral (any implementation runs it through the adapter
contract), and the MIT license on the reference tooling permits every
member category to run, fork, or re-implement without permission.

**What does "executable" mean, concretely?**
A model validates against the rule catalog, answers queries, runs its
processes, and supports reasoning over its graph. The conformance suite's
corpus of valid and invalid documents makes the claim falsifiable by
anyone.
