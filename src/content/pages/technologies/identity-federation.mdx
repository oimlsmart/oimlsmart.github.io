The identity federation gives every participant one account for the whole
estate: the identity service at id.oimlsmart.org is the single OpenID
Provider, and every platform instance is a relying party that runs no
login form and holds no account list of its own. Behind the account sits
the organization registry with the OIML member taxonomy, so a sign-in
resolves to a member state, a corresponding member, an issuing authority,
a test laboratory, or a designated body.

## What you can do today

- **Sign in with what you already hold.** GitHub and Google sign-in are linked today, passkeys and authenticator apps are first-class, and recovery codes cover the loss cases; SMS and voice one-time codes are a documented refusal, because SIM-swap and interception make them weak factors (the capture above).
- **Request an account for your organization.** The [join flow](https://id.oimlsmart.org/op/join) offers the live OIML-CS participants register (the member states, corresponding members, issuing authorities, laboratories, and utilizers on the registry); approval comes from your own organization's administrator, or the BIML secretariat when the organization is not listed yet.
- **Read the machine contract.** The [discovery document](https://id.oimlsmart.org/.well-known/openid-configuration) publishes the endpoints, the algorithms, and the claims every relying party consumes: OIDC Core 1.0, authorization code with PKCE, ES256 ID tokens.
- **Administer your own people.** An organization administrator sets each member's data cone (org-wide, assigned, read-only, or assigned and read-only together), and the token carries the posture so any service learns it without a callback.
- **Give an instrument an identity.** The device class issues each twin a per-device credential speaking the client-credentials grant only, bound to its organization and its instrument model; a re-key or a revocation is a console act with an audit trail.
- **Run your own provider.** The same software runs on a member's own domain as a configuration act, with an executable proof in the repository's end-to-end suite; the [self-host runbook](https://github.com/oimlsmart/identity/blob/main/docs/deployment/identity-self-host.md) is public.

## How it works

<OidcFederation />

Each registered service declares a claims policy and receives the least
claims it needs. The organization registry and its signing keys double as
the estate's [trust registry](/technologies/trust-registry), which is the
identity plane the dataspace work builds on. The service is developed in
the open at [oimlsmart/identity](https://github.com/oimlsmart/identity)
(the source is available; the open-source license is pending, and the
repository says so plainly).

## Why it exists

A certification ecosystem that fragments accounts across services
fragments the record of who did what, and legal metrology runs on
attributed acts: who signed, for which organization, under which
authorization. One provider makes attribution uniform, lets an
organization administer its own people's access, and lets an instrument
hold a machine identity of the same shape as its owner's.

## The honest questions

**Does the estate hold our member list hostage?**
No. The software is the same one the estate runs, the self-host runbook is
public with an executable proof in the end-to-end suite, and a member
running its own provider on its own domain is a configuration act. The
platform instances consume the same claims contract either way, so a
deployment authenticates against the estate provider or the member's own
without a code change.

**Why no SMS codes?**
Because SIM-swap and interception attacks make SMS and voice one-time
codes weak factors, and a certification ecosystem cannot afford weak
attribution. Passkeys, authenticator apps, and recovery codes cover the
same usability ground at a stronger posture; the refusal is documented in
the repository.

**What can a service learn about me?**
Only what its declared claims policy needs. Each relying party receives
the least claims it needs; your organization's administrator, not the
estate, sets your data cone, and the token carries the posture so services
enforce it without a callback to the provider.
