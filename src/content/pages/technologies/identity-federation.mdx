The identity federation gives every participant one account for the whole
estate: the identity service at id.oimlsmart.org is the single OpenID
Provider, and every platform instance is a relying party that runs no
login form and holds no account list of its own. Behind the account sits
the organization registry with the OIML member taxonomy, so a sign-in
resolves to a member state, a corresponding member, an issuing authority,
a test laboratory, or a designated body.

## Why it exists

A certification ecosystem that fragments accounts across services
fragments the record of who did what, and legal metrology runs on
attributed acts: who signed, for which organization, under which
authorization. One provider makes attribution uniform, lets an
organization administer its own people's access, and lets an instrument
hold a machine identity of the same shape as its owner's.

## How it works

The service speaks OpenID Connect Core 1.0 with authorization code and
PKCE, publishes its RFC 8414 discovery document, and issues ES256 ID
tokens. Each registered service declares a claims policy, and receives
the least claims it needs. An organization administrator sets each
member's data cone: org-wide, assigned, read-only, or assigned and
read-only together, and the token carries the posture so any service can
learn it without a callback. Sign-in supports passkeys, authenticator
apps, and recovery codes; SMS and voice one-time codes are a documented
refusal, because SIM-swap and interception make them weak factors.

Upstream sign-in lets a member use an identity they already hold: GitHub
is linked today, Microsoft Entra and national or organizational providers
come next, and adding a provider is a configuration act, not a code
change. Instruments are first-class citizens through the device class:
each twin holds a per-device credential, speaks the client-credentials
grant only, and is bound to its organization and its instrument model, so
a re-key or a revocation is a console act with an audit trail.

## How it interops

The federation is standards-bound: any OpenID Connect relying party can
integrate, and any OpenID Provider built from the same software can serve
a member's own domain. The estate's platform instances consume the same
claims contract, so a member-state deployment authenticates against the
estate provider or against the member's own without a code change. The
organization registry and its signing keys double as the estate's
[trust registry](/technologies/trust-registry), which is the identity
plane the dataspace work builds on.
