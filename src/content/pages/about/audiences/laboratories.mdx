## The world today

A type evaluation in your laboratory means: read the Recommendation's
test procedures from a PDF, set up each condition by hand, record
readings into a form, transcribe the form into a report, and answer
findings about transcription. Your metrologists' expertise goes into
measurement — but a third of the bench time goes into paperwork
mechanics, and every transcription is an error waiting to be found at
the worst moment.

## What changes

The test procedures are **executable processes**, not prose. The
laboratory workbench walks the operator step by step — *condition this,
place that load, record here* — capturing **evidence at the moment of
work**: the condition, the input, the reading, the timestamp, the
instrument's own served value where a twin exists. The report is
**generated** from the accrued evidence, not assembled afterward.
Nothing is transcribed; nothing is re-typed; every row of the report
traces to the probe that produced it and the clause that required it.

## What it looks like in practice

Before a physical instrument ever enters your lab, your operators
rehearse the full procedure on the **simulated SMART twin**: a virtual
load cell with realistic physics — temperature cycles leave residuals,
creep behaves the way R 60 says it should. The workbench instructions
are identical for the simulator and for a real SMART-twin-capable
instrument, so training transfers directly. And the discipline is
honest: where evidence cannot be produced, the verdict reads
`indeterminate` — your laboratory never has to choose between silence
and a pass.

## The proof

The operator guides run the laboratory role end to end against the
simulator — dispatch received, tests executed, evidence accrued, report
issued — with the same screens your bench would use.
