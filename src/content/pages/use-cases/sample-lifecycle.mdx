## One sample, one story

A type evaluation certifies a design, but it tests physical units. The
platform therefore treats every sample as a first-class record with its
own lifecycle: **registered → in transit → received → held → under test
→ returned, retained or disposed.** No step is implied — every movement
is an explicit act by a named party, because a custody chain that
silently updates itself is not a custody chain.

The invariants are simple and the routing is free:

- The applicant registers the samples and ships them where the
  authority directs — to the Issuing Authority, or straight to a test
  laboratory.
- Whoever holds a sample can see it, and everyone in the evaluation can
  see who holds it and where it is.
- A testing laboratory tests the samples assigned to it; the authority
  evaluates against them; and at the end the samples go home to the
  applicant — or are retained for reference, or disposed of, each with
  its reason on the record.

## What each party sees

Every console carries a **Samples** view, scoped to what that party may
see:

| Party | The samples view answers |
|---|---|
| **Applicant** | What to ship, where, and when. Every sample across all applications, its current holder, its tracking link, the returns in transit, and the receipt confirmation when it arrives home. |
| **Issuing Authority** | The requests it issued, the receipts it registered, the samples it holds, the ones forwarded to laboratories, and the closure decisions — return, retain, dispose. |
| **Test Laboratory** | The consignments arriving at the bench, the samples under test, and the return acts when testing completes. |

Open any sample for its **detail page**: the declared particulars, the
photographs and dimensions recorded at receipt, the condition notes,
and the complete custody timeline — every shipment with its carrier and
tracking number, every receipt, every hold, every return, in order.

Every shipment records a carrier and a tracking number, and the number
renders as a link to the platform's public tracking page — the same
parcel-tracking experience a consumer knows, standing on an audited
chain of custody.

## When reality intrudes

The lifecycle has branches, and each branch is a real workflow rather
than an apology:

**A result comes back wrong.** The authority flags the specific result
with a question; the laboratory answers in place, or corrects the value
and resubmits, or re-runs the test — and the report's revision history
preserves the whole exchange.

**A laboratory declines or stalls.** The authority re-dispatches the
same tests to another laboratory with one click; the new request is
linked to the one it replaces, and the coverage view shows what is
complete, running, and not yet dispatched.

**A sample arrives damaged — or vanishes.** The receiving party records
the condition and marks the unit unfit; the authority requests a
replacement; the applicant ships it; and the replacement carries
*replaces* the original while the original carries *replaced by* — the
linkage survives for the audit. A sample lost in transit or in custody,
or a serial that does not match the declaration, follows the same
discipline.

**The samples go home.** Testing done, the laboratory returns the
samples to the authority or directly to the applicant; the authority
returns them to the applicant, retains them with a reason, or disposes
of them with the method recorded; the applicant confirms the arrival —
and the custody chain reads as one continuous story from the first
shipment to the last receipt.

## Why it ends in a gate

An evaluation project cannot be marked complete while any sample's
custody stands open. The samples are accounted for — returned, retained
or disposed, each with its record — or the project is not finished.
That is the doctrine: closure is earned by the record, never
ceremonial.
